DIME Query-Efficient Membership Inference on Diffusion Models

Tue Do  ·  Daniel Alabi

arXiv preprint Blog post GitHub

Abstract

Diffusion models are typically trained on large, sometimes sensitive, image collections, which raises the question of what a trained denoiser retains about any single training example. We study this through membership inference: deciding whether a candidate image was part of the training set. Our starting point is the exact, closed-form MSE-optimal denoiser for a finite training set, which turns out to perform a kind of soft retrieval, reconstructing each noisy query as a posterior-weighted combination of training points. Writing the reconstruction error of that soft retrieval exactly, we split it into two interpretable terms: a reconstruction bias, how far the local posterior mean sits from the candidate, and a local crowding term, how dispersed the plausible training points are around that mean. Bias alone can be fooled by non-members that happen to sit near the center of several training points; crowding exposes exactly that ambiguity. Both terms are estimated from forward queries to the denoising network, and the cheapest variant of our attack, DIME, needs only two queries per candidate. Across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL10-U, CelebA, and Guided Diffusion on ImageNet, DIME improves detection at low false-positive rates over prior attacks at comparable or substantially lower query cost, with its two-query variant outperforming existing thirty-query baselines on several settings. Against differentially private training, DIME's advantage collapses back toward chance along with prior methods, consistent with DP's intended guarantee.

Reconstruction is retrieval

The exact finite-set optimal denoiser is a soft nearest-neighbor decoder: every training point gets a posterior responsibility for a given noisy query, and the model's implicit reconstruction is the responsibility-weighted mean of the training set.

Reconstruction error from this retrieval splits exactly into bias (distance from the posterior mean to the candidate) and crowding (dispersion of the responsible points). DIME estimates both from as few as two forward queries to the network.

Results

Illustrative summaries of the experiments in the paper — see the preprint for full numbers, checkpoints, and datasets.

Low-FPR detection

DIME's largest gains appear at the low-false-positive operating point that matters most for credible membership decisions, improving certain metrics by a factor of up to 3× over prior attacks.

Query efficiency

Bias and crowding are estimated from the same perturbed queries, so DIME's cheapest variant needs only two total queries — and can outperform prior 30-query baselines.

Stability across timesteps

Smaller DDPM checkpoints stay effective across a broad noise range, while the larger ImageNet Guided Diffusion model is most vulnerable at early, low-noise timesteps.

Under differential privacy

Against differentially private training, DIME's advantage — like prior attacks' — is driven back toward chance, consistent with what DP is meant to guarantee.
